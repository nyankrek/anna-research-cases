A vocabulary for
product perception.

How do you ask people what a product feels like, beyond whether they can use it?

SCROLL TO EXPLORE ↓
usefulcleartrustworthyconfusingenjoyable

THE QUESTION

Give teams a more precise language for an experience.

Product teams were asking whether their interfaces felt old, complicated or appealing. A translated version of the Microsoft Desirability Toolkit was already in use, but its words had not been adapted or tested for this Russian-language product context.

MY ROLEResearch design & facilitation
METHODSToolkit adaptation, team workshops, surveys
OUTPUTAn adapted desirability word list
01 / CONTEXT

Usability did not tell the whole story.

Behavioral metrics and task success could show what people did. They were less helpful when a team wanted to understand the qualities people associated with the product. The vocabulary itself needed research before we could rely on it in later studies.

02 / APPROACH

Build the words with people, then test them.

I reviewed the original toolkit, organised the adaptation process and ran two survey iterations. Designers and a UX writer helped select terms; users described the best and worst social networks; active users then used the refined list to describe our product and three competitors.

  1. 01Translate and classify the original terms
  2. 02Review relevance with design and writing partners
  3. 03Narrow positive and negative terms with users
  4. 04Add neutral terms and compare four products
03 / OUTCOME

A reusable research instrument.

The work produced a word list better suited to the audience, language and product. Testing it with users and colleagues gave teams a more consistent way to explore emotional and aesthetic responses in future research.

THE VALUE

A shared vocabulary made a subjective question easier to discuss and investigate.
